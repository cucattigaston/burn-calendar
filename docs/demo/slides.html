Two sensors, one fire record that doesn’t add up

Naive: MODIS until 2011, raw VIIRS afterHarmonized

VIIRS has 375 m pixels against 1 km for MODIS, and different overpass times. In Argentina it reports 3.2× as many hotspots for the same fires.

Joining the records as they are invents a fire boom in 2012: the count jumps from 73k to 169k.

MODIS on Terra and Aqua is being retired in 2026–27. Without harmonization, every future year loses its 25-year baseline.

From two sensors to MODIS-equivalent hotspots

  1. 1
    CleanMODIS confidence ≥ 30; VIIRS nominal or high. Keep only vegetation and land fires, dropping volcanoes, industrial sources and offshore detections (FIRMS “type” flag).
  2. 2
    Put both on one gridCount hotspots in 0.1° cells by month, separately for day and night overpasses.
  3. 3
    Learn conversion factors where both sensors overlapMODIS ÷ VIIRS for every 2° region and overpass over 2012–2021, before the end-of-mission orbit drift. Shrunk toward the national factor (day 0.37, night 0.15) where fires are few. Terra-only months of 2000–02 are scaled to Terra + Aqua the same way.
  4. 4
    Join the erasMODIS observed until 2011, then VIIRS converted (2026 from near-real-time data). Days when a satellite was down are filled by scaling to the full month and flagged. The sensor of origin no longer matters.

A baseline that survives the end of MODIS

  • Emergency managers and fire brigades: see when an area usually burns and whether this month is unusual. August 2026 ran at 20% of a normal August nationwide, a drop NOAA-20 confirms
  • Scientists: 25 consistent years to study trends and anomalies
  • Land managers and CONAE: an open, documented method that runs on public data
