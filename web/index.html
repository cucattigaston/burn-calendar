How the two sensors become one record

MODIS (Terra and Aqua, 1 km pixels) has watched fires since 2000 but is being retired. VIIRS (375 m pixels) continues the record with more detail, so it reports several times more hotspots for the same fires. Counting both as they are would invent a fire boom in 2012.

This tool expresses everything in MODIS-equivalent hotspots. VIIRS counts are converted with conversion factors learned where both sensors overlapped (2012–2021), separately for each 2° region and for day and night passes. Regions with little data lean on the national factor. The early Terra-only months (2000–mid 2002) are scaled the same way to match Terra + Aqua.

On days when a satellite sent no data at all, such as Terra in mid-2001 or S-NPP in 2022, 2024 and 2026, the month is scaled to its full length. Those months carry a small white dot in the calendar. Months from August 2026 use near-real-time data, which FIRMS may still reprocess.

Each conversion was tested by hiding one year at a time, learning the factors from the others, and predicting the hidden year.

See it for yourself. Set “Series shown” to Without harmonization to see the false jump in 2012. In the panel Test it yourself: hide a year you can re-run a simplified version of the validation for any area.

How sure is each number? Converted months show a likely range, which covers the true MODIS-equivalent value 8 times out of 10. The ranges come from the same hide-one-year test. For each size of value, from a single 0.1° cell to the whole country, we measured how far predictions landed from what MODIS really counted. Small numbers are noisy: an estimate of 10 hotspots means somewhere between 4 and 16. Large ones are tight: an estimate of 1,000 means 730 to 1,150. In the “Compared with normal” view, hatched months could still be normal once that range is taken into account. Months observed directly by MODIS (July 2002 to 2011) carry no conversion uncertainty.
